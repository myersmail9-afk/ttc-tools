Always ask a client for the distance around the trunk, never the diameter. Wrapping a tape around has one obvious way to do it; measuring across by eye gives a chord, not a diameter, and reads small. Mixing the two up is a 3× error.

Getting the measurement without going out

Roughly a third of the trees on this list have no diameter on file, and a client may well point at a different tree than the one we treated. A photo shows the foliage but has no scale in it, so it cannot set a price. One number can.

the line to useWrap a tape measure around the trunk at about chest height and tell me the inches around — around, not across. If the tree splits into several trunks, do each one separately. I'll work the rest out from there.

What to say on the phone

Open with the tree, not the offer. “Joseph at Total Tree Care. Calling about the 22″ silver maple on your corner — we injected it for iron in 2023. Two minutes?”

  1. Did it respond? Did it green up. Did it hold.
  2. Are the symptoms back? Pale leaves with green veins, worst on the new growth up top. Any dieback in the upper canopy.
  3. Quote it on the call. “No visit needed, we still have the diameter.” Say the number.
  4. No diameter on file? “Wrap a tape around the trunk at chest height and tell me the inches around.” Never ask for the diameter. Price it the same day.
  5. Different tree? Fine — measure that one. Two trees is a better job than one.
  6. Should it be treated? Symptoms plus a healthy tree, yes. A hard-stressed tree does not get injected. Say so out loud.
  7. Cambistat also due? “Growth regulator from that year is on the same cycle — one visit covers both.”
  8. No thanks? Offer spring. Soil injection runs March–April and keeps them in the book.

Why fall, and why now

  • Inject before leaf drop. The tree pulls the iron down as it goes dormant, so it is loaded for spring. The window is roughly 15 Sep – 15 Oct.
  • A macro injection holds two to three years. Anything treated 2024 or earlier is at or past the end of its cycle.
  • No bid visit. We have the diameter from last time, so the price is arithmetic. If a client really wants eyes on it, Trevor measures on a PHC day and can treat the same trip on approval.
  • The photo is the honesty check, not the price check — it tells us whether the tree actually needs another round. If it is holding, we leave it alone.
