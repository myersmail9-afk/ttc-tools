Cabling and Bracing

We install cabling and bracing systems for trees throughout Logan and the surrounding Cache Valley towns.

Signs You May Need This

  • Two trunks or main limbs growing pressed together, forming a tight V instead of a U
  • A crack, split, or bark pinched inside where two branches grow together
  • You want to keep a tree with a structural weakness rather than remove it
  • A branch or leader broke in a storm and the rest of the tree is still standing

What's Included

  • A weight-reduction prune first, to ease the load on the weak union
  • A bracing rod through the union for added support, if pruning alone is not enough
  • A cable set high in the canopy for larger trees that need more support
  • We do not recommend a strap wrapped around the union. A strap sits on the tree's vascular tissue just under the bark and can girdle it, cutting off nutrients to everything above

How It Works

  1. Request a free estimate.
  2. An ISA Certified Arborist visits your property and writes your estimate.
  3. We schedule the work and get it done.

What to Expect on the Day

  • We assess whether the union can be supported before recommending cabling or bracing over removal
  • We take the least invasive step that does the job: pruning first, then a brace, then a cable if the tree is large enough to need one

About Cost

Every cabling and bracing job starts with a free estimate. Not every defect needs the same fix. What drives the price is which step of support the tree needs (pruning, a brace, or a cable) and the tree's size.

Frequently Asked Questions

Does a crack or split mean the tree has to come down?
Not always. Two trunks growing pressed together, or a crack at a branch union, is a structural defect, but there is a range of options short of removal: weight-reduction pruning, a bracing rod, or a cable high in the canopy for larger trees.
Can I just strap the branches together myself?
We don't recommend it. A ratchet strap sits right on the tree's vascular tissue, just under the bark, and can girdle the trunk, cutting off nutrients to the growth above the strap. A bracing rod through the union looks more invasive but is the safer method.
Does cabling and bracing actually hold up in a bad storm?
In David's experience, properly braced and cabled trees held through the severe Cache Valley windstorms of September 2020 and October 2021.
Is a cabled or braced tree completely safe?
No tree is risk-free once it's standing. Cabling and bracing mitigate the risk of a weak union failing; they don't eliminate it.
