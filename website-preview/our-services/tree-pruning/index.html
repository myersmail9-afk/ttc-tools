Signs You May Need This

  • Dead, broken, crossing, or diseased branches in the canopy
  • Branches hanging over your roof, driveway, or walkway
  • It has been several years since the tree was last pruned
  • A young tree with multiple tops or crowded growth
  • You want more light, view, or clearance around the tree

What's Included

  • A written estimate before any work begins
  • A general prune that removes anything dead, broken, crossing, or diseased, while the tree's overall height and shape stay about the same
  • A little weight taken off heavily loaded side branches to ease the load on the tree
  • A certified arborist on site for every pruning job, so the work meets industry standards
  • Full cleanup

Shrub and Hedge Pruning

We also shear and shape shrubs and hedges. For a lilac, our standard approach is to go to the base and remove about a third of the stems, taking the biggest ones out first, then hand-prune the rest down to size. On other shrubs, a natural hand-prune (many small cuts, rather than shearing) takes more time than shearing, but it is worth it for a healthier, more natural-looking shrub.

Fruit Tree Pruning

Fruit trees get pruned every year, not every few years like ornamental trees. The style is more aggressive, and the tree regrows hard and upward, so an annual visit keeps it in check. The best window is any time after the leaves drop, through the winter. If you are growing the tree for fruit, we can also reduce its height, which is a typical move for better fruit production. Have a fruit tree that also needs spray protection? See our Fruit Tree Spraying page.

Young Tree Structural Pruning

Structural pruning while a tree is young directs its growth and heads off structural defects before they start. Like raising a child, a little correction while a tree is young is worth far more than a lot of correction once it is old. We recommend pruning a young tree annually for about its first five years, then every other year, then every three to four years once it matures.

Best Time of Year

Mature trees do best on a repeating cycle. We recommend pruning a mature tree about every three to four years; young trees benefit from pruning more often while they establish. Fruit trees are the exception. They are pruned every year, any time after leaf drop through winter.

About Cost

Every pruning job starts with a free estimate. Price depends on the tree's size, how many branches need attention, and access to the tree. We do not guarantee pruning, because a tree is a living thing and conditions change over time.

Frequently Asked Questions

How often should my tree be pruned?
It depends on the tree. A mature tree usually does well pruned every three to four years. A young tree benefits from pruning annually for its first five years or so, then every other year. Fruit trees are pruned every year.
Will pruning change how my tree looks?
Not with a general prune. The height and shape of the tree stay about the same. We remove dead, broken, crossing, or diseased wood and address anything specific you want, like clearance over a driveway.
Do you top trees?
No. We follow the ANSI A300 pruning standard and do not top trees.
Can you trim branches away from the power line?
That specific work is called line clearance, and it requires a line-clearance certified arborist. We refer that work to the power company, which does it at no charge. We can still prune the rest of the tree.
Do you prune shrubs and hedges too?
Yes. See Shrub and Hedge Pruning above.
