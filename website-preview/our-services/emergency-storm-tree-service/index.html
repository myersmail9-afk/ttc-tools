Emergency and Storm Tree Service

We respond to storm-damaged and hazard trees for homes and businesses in Logan and across Cache and Box Elder Counties.

If a Tree Is on a Power Line

Stay clear of downed or sagging power lines and call 911 first. Treat any line touching a tree as live, even if it looks dead. Once the site is safe, call our office and we will help with the tree.

Signs You May Need This

  • A tree or large limb has come down in a storm and is on your house, garage, fence, car, or yard
  • A branch is cracked, hanging, or caught in the tree above something it could fall on
  • A tree is leaning more than it used to, or the ground around its base looks heaved or cracked open
  • A dead or dying tree is standing near your house, driveway, or a spot where people spend time
  • A tree was struck by lightning and has cracked bark, a split trunk, or a section that looks blown open

What We Do

  • Assess the tree and the hazard, and tell you plainly what we're looking at
  • When we have someone available, we can often get the immediate hazard on the ground the same visit, then follow up with a full quote for the rest of the work
  • Full cleanup, with trunk wood hauled off unless you ask us to leave firewood
  • Crane removal when a tree is too large or too hard to access to take down safely by hand
  • After storm or lightning damage, we look at the whole tree, not just what broke. A tree that already failed once at a weak point is more likely to fail there again in the next storm

How It Works

  1. Call our office or request a free estimate.
  2. We talk through what you're seeing and get you on the schedule.
  3. An ISA Certified Arborist assesses the tree and we get the work done.

What to Expect

  • After large storms, calls can pile up quickly across the valley. We do our best to get to you as soon as we can, especially when your property is at risk
  • If the immediate hazard needs to come down right away and we have someone available, we can often handle that on the first visit, then quote the remaining work separately
  • If we need to drive on your lawn, we lay down ground mats to protect it
  • Full cleanup when the work is done

About Cost

Every emergency and storm call starts with a conversation about what you're seeing. What drives the price is the size of the tree or limb, how hard it is to access, and whether a crane is needed. Emergency work, and any work you need done within two weeks, is billed at our emergency rate. We will walk you through the price before any work begins.

Frequently Asked Questions

A tree looks like it's going to fall on my house. Do you do emergency jobs?
Yes. After large storms the work can pile up quickly across the valley, but we do our best to get to you as soon as we can, especially when your property is at risk.
Do you take calls after hours?
For emergencies, we try to. Call (435) 752-1884. After-hours emergency work is billed at our emergency rate.
What should I do if a tree or branch is on a power line?
Stay clear of the line and call 911 first. Treat any line touching a tree as live, even if it looks dead or the power seems to be out. Call our office once the site is safe.
Can you take the hazard down the same day you come out?
When we have someone available, yes, we can often put the immediate hazard on the ground the same visit, then follow up with a full quote for the rest of the work.
My tree fell over and pulled up a section of the yard with it. Is that normal?
Yes. When a tree fails at the root instead of the trunk, it typically heaves up a plate of soil and roots. That heaved, spongy ground is a normal part of how a tree like that comes down.
Do you handle cleanup, or just the hazard cut?
Full cleanup is included in the work. Any branch or trunk wood we cut gets hauled away, unless you'd like us to leave firewood.
My tree was struck by lightning. What now?
Call us and we'll come look at it. A true lightning strike is less common than people think, and a lot of storm damage that looks dramatic turns out fine structurally. An ISA Certified Arborist can tell you what you're actually looking at before any pruning happens.
